## The big trends

### The industry talks about software *factories* now

Agentic, automated development moved forward a lot this year. Speakers now talk about **factories**: light factory, grey factory, dark factory — how much of the work runs without a human watching. Even well-known developers say they have not written a line of code by hand for six months.

Background reading: [The five levels, from spicy autocomplete to the software factory](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/) (Dan Shapiro).

### Developers risk becoming "meat proxies"

When the agent writes the code and the developer only reviews it, the human becomes a *meat proxy*: a body that approves what the machine produced. That is a real loss of identity for developers. But the shift also goes towards the **functional side**: less pure technique, more of the product and the business. In my view, that brings us closer to the people we build for — as long as we keep the role of steering, not just reviewing.

### No revolution this year

There is a certain lack of novelty: no big new concept appeared. Models are stronger and people sharpen their tools, but nothing changed at the technical level.

### Agentic features inside applications

Adding agents *inside* our applications is a big trend too. Nothing very new, but it keeps growing. For now, at SFPD we only use agentic AI to *build* our software, not yet *inside* our applications.

## Invest in your harness

The harness is everything around the agent that helps it do correct work: **documentation, tests, skills, rules, feedback loops**. Improving it is the big trend of the week, and I think this is the way we must go.

> Invest in your harness — your results will thank you.

- **Own your harness**: write your own skills instead of only consuming other people's.

## My takeaways

- **Most of the code isn't written by hand anymore.** The job moves to specifying, steering and reviewing.
- **Vibe coding is for pet projects.** Building something production-ready needs a harness.
- **Review is about risk.** Review means identifying the risks and looking at what really matters, not reading every line.
- **LLM as a judge** is worth having, with limits: it helps to write tests and to ease the review, but it doesn't replace a human decision on risk.
- **Open question:** are loops really a thing? With a good harness (docs, tests, rules), the agent should get it right quickly, so the loop should stay small.

## Pitfalls

- **Humanizing AI doesn't work.** Trying to reproduce human behaviour with persona agents ("the architect", "the PM"…) does not work — don't copy human processes onto agents.

## Tools & tips

### MCP

- **Java MCP** (javadocs.dev): gives the agent the Javadoc and sources of your dependencies, so it stops unzipping JARs. Endpoint: `https://www.javadocs.dev/mcp`

### Skills

- **`/grill-me`** and **`/grill-me-with-doc`** from Matt Pocock: the agent interviews you until the plan is clear. [Setup guide](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-setup-matt-pocock-skills)

### Commands

- **`/goal`**: let the agent loop until a goal is reached.

### Tools

- **[RTK](https://www.rtk-ai.app/)**: cuts the tokens a coding agent spends on command output.
- docker sandbox https://www.docker.com/products/docker-sandboxes/



### Spec-Driven Development frameworks

Pick one framework and use it. Above all, change it to fit what you want to do: take ideas from it, modify it and make it your own.

- **OpenSpec**: change proposals on top of living specs. Designed for brownfield and changes to existing code. [openspec.dev](https://openspec.dev)
- **GitHub Spec Kit**: step-by-step flow (constitution, specify, plan, tasks, implement). [github.com/github/spec-kit](https://github.com/github/spec-kit)
- **Kiro (AWS)**: spec-centric IDE, a VS Code fork by Amazon. [kiro.dev](https://kiro.dev)
- **BMAD**: a team of agent personas (analyst, PM, architect, dev). Produces a lot of .md. [docs.bmad-method.org](https://docs.bmad-method.org)
- **AI Unified Process**: built on use cases, aimed at enterprise, brownfield, long-lived systems. [unifiedprocess.ai](https://unifiedprocess.ai)
- **Tessl**: a skills registry for sharing versioned specs and skills. [tessl.io](https://tessl.io)
- **Matt Pocock's skills**: pragmatic, hand-crafted skills (grill-me, to-prd, wayfinder…). [github.com/mattpocock/skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills)
- **Superpowers**: brainstorming, multi-agent and adversarial review. [github.com/obra/superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers)
- **GSD (Get Shit Done)**: a phase loop with each phase run in fresh-context subagents. [github.com/open-gsd/gsd-core](https://github.com/open-gsd/gsd-core)
